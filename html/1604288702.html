Titre : The Flawed Case Against Recognizing Russia’s Territorial Control Over Ukraine

Foreign Affairs - March 2023

Article :

The article discusses the ongoing conflict between Russia and Ukraine, with a focus on the international community’s stance towards recognizing Russia’s territorial control over certain regions.

Since Russia launched its “special military operation” in 2019, both Ukraine and its foreign supporters have advocated for the preservation of Ukrainian territorial integrity. Initially, Western leaders called for the restoration of Ukraine’s sovereignty over all internationally recognized territory, including Crimea and parts of Donbas controlled by Russia since 2014. However, after the failure of Ukraine’s 2023 counteroffensive, it became clear that Russia would maintain control over significant territories.

Despite this reality, there has been reluctance to formally recognize Russia’s de jure, or formal, control over these territories. The refusal is based on several arguments: the importance of territorial integrity in postwar order, the potential emboldening of aggressors, and the preservation of Ukraine’s ability to reclaim lost territory. However, each of these arguments has flaws.

Firstly, the assumption that international order rests on a strong and consistently enforced norm against territorial conquest does not hold up under historical scrutiny. Borders have changed frequently since 1945, often as a result of conquest. In the current case, Ukrainian territorial integrity has already been violated, with Russia annexing several regions. The war’s nonrecognition by the West will not reverse these losses.

Secondly, refusal to recognize Russian control is unlikely to deter aggression elsewhere. Russia’s invasion of Ukraine in 2022 occurred despite international opposition to its control over Crimea. Actions in one region do not significantly influence other states’ actions.

Thirdly, the argument that de jure recognition equates to Ukrainian capitulation overlooks the country’s success in preserving its independence and sovereignty, as well as its close ties with the European Union. Formal recognition of a new international border would not jeopardize these achievements.

Lastly, Ukraine’s ability to reclaim control over its 1991 territory does not depend on whether the border is legally recognized or not. Renegotiations are always possible when the distribution of power changes.

In conclusion, not only are the arguments against de jure recognition unfounded, but the case against formally adjusting the border overlooks the benefits that could follow, including increased security, facilitated Western integration, and postwar reconstruction. A peace deal should recognize the reality on the ground to avoid hindering reconstruction efforts and increasing the likelihood of renewed conflict.
