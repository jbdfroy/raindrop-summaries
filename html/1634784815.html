    Title: The Dangers and Challenges of the 2025 U.S.-China Trade Truce

    Introduction: The 2025 U.S.-China trade truce, following the meetings in Busan, marked a significant step in resetting relations between the two superpowers. However, the agreement is not without weaknesses and external pressures that could potentially derail its success.

    Content:

    1. External Pressures: The truce faces challenges from various regions such as Europe, the Middle East, and Latin America. A confident China may push back against U.S. measures in these regions, escalating tensions and creating frictions.

    2. Transshipment Tariffs: The issue of transshipment tariffs, which the U.S. has threatened to levy on third countries importing intermediate Chinese goods for final products exported to the U.S., was not addressed in the official statements of the Busan meetings. This omission could potentially rekindle the economic tensions temporarily tamped down by the truce.

    3. Rare Earths and Fentanyl: Although Beijing agreed to renew exports of rare earths and tighten restrictions on the export of fentanyl precursors, the two countries did not specify exact timelines or clear criteria for progress. This lack of clarity could lead to disappointment on both sides and potential restoration of higher U.S. tariff levels on imports from China.

    4. Semiconductors: The U.S. and China have long-running differences on Taiwan, and Beijing may push for changes in U.S. policy on cross-strait relations. If Washington fails to meet China’s expectations on technology concessions, especially semiconductors, China may be less willing to faithfully implement or extend the commitments on rare earths and fentanyl that it made in Busan.

    5. Arms Sales to Taiwan: The upcoming summit is expected to address the issue of Taiwan, and China may push for revisions in U.S. policy that could undercut Taiwan’s morale. A large new U.S. arms sales commitment to Taiwan in 2026 could further escalate bilateral tensions.

    Conclusion: The 2025 U.S.-China trade truce was an important step, but various potential sources of tension remain unaddressed. These unresolved issues could derail the gains of the Busan accord and further escalate bilateral tensions.
