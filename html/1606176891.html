    This text appears to be an analysis on the lessons that the United States military can learn from the ongoing conflict in Ukraine between Russia and Ukraine. The author highlights that the Ukrainian forces have shown resilience against a stronger Russian military due to their adaptability and use of low-cost, easily producible equipment such as drones and munitions.

    The text suggests that the U.S. military should focus on:

    1. Simulating realistic war games that mimic the fog of war with incomplete information, degraded command-and-control systems, constrained logistics, and political limits.
    2. Red teaming to think up situations the U.S. might miss and reconstitute the Office of Net Assessment for independent long-range military assessments.
    3. Encouraging frequent field exercises that stress test units in contested environments to improve improvisational skills.
    4. Mandating that troops develop asymmetric tactics during these exercises, especially when role-playing as the adversary.
    5. Designing and procuring modular systems made up of commercially available components that can be easily replaced and upgraded.
    6. Investing in advanced manufacturing technologies such as 3D printing to speed up production of equipment in the field.
    7. Increasing investments in drone component companies and stockpiling drones, drone parts, and munitions to avoid running out during prolonged wars.
    8. Reallocating some of the budget currently dedicated to developing new exquisite platforms to buying ships, tanks, planes, software, or any other kind of kit for troops.
    9. Preparing to iterate rapidly and producing large quantities of low-cost capabilities.

    Overall, the text emphasizes that the U.S. military needs to adopt a mindset of quantity having a quality all its own, similar to World War II when the U.S. dramatically accelerated military manufacturing. The U.S. should focus on improving adaptability, improvisational skills, and resupply capabilities, as well as producing modular systems and adopting advanced manufacturing technologies for speedier production of equipment in the field.
