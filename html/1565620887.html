    Titre : ‘Potential Deal Between US, Denmark, and Greenland Over Strategic Island’ - Source : Foreign Policy Magazine (September 2023) - Article :

    The recent statement by US President Donald Trump about a potential future deal with Greenland over its strategic resources has caught the attention of international observers. The announcement, made on social media, comes after Trump’s previous threats to seize the island using military force.

    At Davos, however, Trump appeared to have taken military seizure off the table and put tariffs on hold following “satisfactory” talks with NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte. The details of this potential deal remain unclear.

    Greenland is strategically important for US national security in the Arctic, with significant mineral deposits that could bolster US industrial and technological supply chains. The United States has had a military presence in Greenland since World War II, as stipulated in a defense agreement signed with Denmark in 1951.

    A deal between the three nations would need to be diplomatic and respectful of Greenland’s sovereignty and territorial integrity. Recent surveys show that few Americans support a military takeover of Greenland, and only a tiny proportion of Greenlanders want to join the United States.

    The US can claim significant economic and security benefits from closer ties with Greenland and Denmark. The countries have been deepening their relationship for years, and Trump can leverage existing wins in areas such as infrastructure development, mineral mapping, and preventing foreign influence from China and Russia.

    Such a deal must be facilitated through institutional engagement, including involving the US Congress to give it legal permanence. It is crucial that any agreement respects Greenland’s sovereignty and territorial integrity while addressing US concerns about access and basing rights.

    The deal should go beyond military engagements and focus on new investments and commercial initiatives, such as incentivizing the Greenlandic fishing industry to shift its exports to Europe and the United States, and establishing maritime routes for tourism and commerce between Greenland and Alaska and Maine.

    To consolidate economic interdependence, the US should offer special import carve-outs for Danish and Greenlandic goods, create new maritime routes, and implement specialized labor arrangements to offset labor shortages and increase mutual trust. The deal could also include provisions that exclude Chinese and Russian investments in critical infrastructure in Greenland, either explicitly or through references to national security considerations.

    The negotiation process should be coordinated with NATO to align with Trump’s desire for greater burden sharing within the alliance. A stable and prosperous Denmark and Greenland, within a US-secured Arctic, can be an enormous asset to the United States, deterring Russian and Chinese military adventurism.
