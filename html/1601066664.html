Titre : Whitaker’s Remarks Signal U.S. Disengagement from NATO Overseen by Trump Administration

Foreign Affairs (January 2023)

Article :

Last November, Matthew Whitaker, the U.S. ambassador to NATO, shocked European officials at the Berlin Security Conference by suggesting that Germany could take over the supreme allied commander position. Although Whitaker acknowledged that such a transition was not imminent, his remarks were indicative of a broader pattern of U.S. disengagement from NATO under the Trump administration.

The withdrawal symptoms include ceding the leading role in providing conventional forces and a possible division of conventional and nuclear duties between Europe and the United States. This could revive the idea, broadly unpopular outside Washington, of splitting the roles, which was unworkable during the Cold War due to the need for sustained U.S. presence and deterrence.

For years, successive U.S. administrations from both parties have valued being first among equals within NATO and retaining overreaching authority for military planning and operations. Yet, Trump’s administration has come to believe that the United States is better off going it alone, which could prove detrimental to U.S. national security.

Offloading many of SHAPE’s defense planning responsibilities to non-American officers would carry profound strategic, operational, and tactical implications for the alliance—and for the United States itself. A non-American supreme allied commander would mean a non-American issuing operational orders to U.S. officers in a European contingency, an arrangement the Pentagon has long regarded as a nonstarter.

The Trump administration may not realize the difficulties of shedding responsibilities without surrendering control, as seen in examples like the U.S.-South Korean military alliance, where plans to transfer operational control have been advanced intermittently but remain largely intact with U.S. leadership. Shifting the United States from a leadership role to a supporting one in an alliance of 31 other members will be infinitely harder.

The quiet disengagement from its role as alliance manager, honed over decades to the shared benefit of Washington and Europe, will close the book on nearly a century of productive partnership, permanently weakening the United States in the process.
