Sommaire

    This text discusses the potential succession crisis in Russia following Vladimir Putin’s departure from power, drawing parallels with the Soviet Union’s struggle after Joseph Stalin’s death in 1953. The piece highlights the importance of the “selectorate” – a small inner circle occupying central state offices who can compete for power and influence the choice of the next leader.

    The text explains that, like after Stalin’s death, there will likely be a competition among key figures holding senior positions, with individuals possessing both formal authority and informal ties being the most likely to be part of the post-Putin selectorate. These individuals include high-ranking officials such as speakers of parliament, prime minister, head of the Presidential Administration, secretaries of the State and Security Councils, head of the security service (FSB), minister of defense, and others.

    The article also mentions that continued stagnation in Putin’s regime has led to a delay in natural progression for potential leaders, resulting in friction among members of Russia’s political class. It suggests that this could lead to intergenerational conflict and potentially chaos during the transition to a new leader.

    Finally, the text emphasizes that the longer Putin’s gerontocratic elites remain at the top of Russia’s most powerful institutions, the less likely an orderly transition to the next president will be. The pressure is mounting for individuals to exploit any opening, potentially leading to instability and missteps during the leadership transition process.
