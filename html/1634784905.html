    Title: The Trump Administration’s New Approach to War: A Departure from the Powell Doctrine

    Source: Foreign Affairs (March 2022)

    Article:

    In contrast to the Powell Doctrine, a strategy developed during the Gulf War that emphasizes clear objectives, overwhelming force, and public support, the Trump administration has pursued a new approach to war. This approach, characterized by flexibility, ambiguity, and limited force, has been evident in several conflicts, including strikes against Syria, military operations against the Islamic State (ISIS), the assassination of Iranian General Qasem Soleimani, and the war in Yemen.

    The Powell Doctrine, named after General Colin Powell who later served as Secretary of State, advocates for force as a last resort after all nonviolent means have been exhausted. It emphasizes a clear objective, a clear exit strategy, public support, and the use of overwhelming, decisive force. However, the Trump administration has prioritized flexibility and avoids public debate and congressional authorization for conflicts.

    One departure from the Powell Doctrine is the use of ambiguity as a source of advantage, with Trump launching attacks without public ultimatums or clear objectives. The administration has also not sought to win public support or congressional approval for its conflicts. Instead, each conflict has begun suddenly and followed an unpredictable course.

    Another aspect of the Powell Doctrine that Trump seems to have done away with is the emphasis on public support. Rather than making a case for each war, Trump often insists he hopes to avoid it. The president’s administration puts a priority on surprise and has largely sidelined Congress.

    The Trump administration has also avoided articulating clear objectives for its use of force. When announcing attacks on Iran, Trump said the objective was to defend American people by eliminating imminent threats from the Iranian regime, but Tehran was neither enriching uranium nor in possession of missiles capable of reaching the United States.

    In some ways, the Trump response to the Powell Doctrine has served recent history better than a dogmatic application of the original. However, the limits of Trump’s way of war may soon be clear, especially in the context of the ongoing conflict in Iran. If the United States and Israel are unsuccessful in toppling the Iranian regime, Trump could declare victory by claiming that the objective was, from the beginning, to simply weaken Iran and to ensure that it does not obtain a nuclear weapon. Such a scenario would demonstrate one more limitation of the Trump approach: it does not pave the way for long-term peace but postpones conflict to another day.
