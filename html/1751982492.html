    Synthesis of the article:

    The text discusses the evolution of Turkey’s foreign policy under President Recep Tayyip Erdogan, focusing on his pursuit of strategic autonomy through a close relationship with Russia, and its impact on Turkey’s relations with NATO and Europe. Initially, Erdogan distanced Turkey from Western allies to forge ties with Russia, culminating in the purchase of S-400 missile defense systems despite objections from NATO. However, domestic and regional developments led Erdogan to reassess his approach, recognizing the need to strengthen ties with Europe and the United States.

    Economic challenges, including inflation, a collapsing currency, balance-of-payments crisis, and the devastating earthquake in 2023, as well as political pressures necessitated a change in perception of Turkey. Erdogan appointed Mehmet Simsek to lead the economy and signaled a move towards closer diplomatic alignment with Western partners, supporting efforts to revive Turkey’s EU membership bid and lifting objections to Sweden’s NATO membership.

    Turkey also took steps to reduce its dependence on Russian energy, negotiating deals with Iran, the United States, and other non-Russian suppliers, and shelving plans for a Russian gas hub in Turkey. The nuclear partnership with Russia has come under strain due to delays in the Akkuyu nuclear plant project and the pursuit of alternative projects with the United States and South Korea.

    Regional developments, such as the fall of Assad’s regime in Syria, have helped Turkey strengthen its partnerships with NATO allies, particularly in defense, as Turkey’s military modernization program relies on acquisitions from these allies. Ankara has also expressed an interest in participating in Europe’s efforts to build up its own defense industry.

    Despite this shift towards the West, Turkey still aims to maintain strategic autonomy by engaging with multiple actors, including China, Russia, and regional powers. However, Erdogan now understands that working with the United States and Europe strengthens Turkey’s position in pursuing its interests abroad. The article concludes by highlighting the ongoing tensions between Turkey and Russia, particularly regarding Turkey’s mediating role in the Russia-Ukraine conflict and its support for Ukraine’s aspirations to join NATO.
