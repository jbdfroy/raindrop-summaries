    Titre: Joint US-Israeli Military Operation Against Iran: Escalating Tensions and Uncertain Outcomes

    Source: Foreign Policy (February 28, 2023)

    Article:

    The United States and Israel have conducted a joint military operation against Iran, targeting Iranian leadership and capabilities. This operation, dubbed Operation Epic Fury, has raised concerns about the potential for escalation, as Iran has warned it will retaliate.

    Last June, the focus of US strikes was on Iran’s nuclear program, hitting three key nuclear facilities. This time, the objectives are less clear, and the path to de-escalation is not evident. Operation Midnight Hammer, the previous attack, was limited, with Iran’s response predictable. In contrast, Operation Epic Fury has opened a Pandora’s Box, with no clear objective within reach.

    Tensions have been mounting since late December, when Iranian protesters flooded the streets, initially over the collapsing national currency, but quickly expanding around the country with calls for the regime’s demise. The regime responded with an intensely bloody campaign to quell the protests, killing several thousand.

    Trump’s response was economic and personal, engaging Elon Musk to help counter Tehran’s internet blackout, imposing tariffs on trade with the Islamic Republic, and adding US sanctions against Iranian “shadow banking” networks and regime officials. Trump also called on Iranians to “keep protesting—take over institutions.”

    Tehran sought to deter US intervention with threats of its own, suggesting that US troops and assets throughout the region would be in the cross hairs. The regime moved with alarming alacrity to kill both thousands of its own people and the movement’s swelling momentum, raising concerns about the regime’s resilience under external pressure.

    As tensions mounted, US allies and partners in the region entreated Washington not to act too hastily, fearing Iranian retaliation. In mid-January, the US boosted its military assets in the region, including two aircraft carrier groups and scores of aircraft—a buildup in scale and scope not seen since the Iraq war more than two decades ago.

    A flurry of calls and engagements among regional intermediaries sought to jump-start diplomacy that had been largely stagnant since last year’s 12-day war. While these were able to narrow some gaps, stubborn points of divergence remained, especially on Iranian nuclear concessions and on US sanctions relief.

    The risk for escalation is still very high, and a US climbdown appears less likely in response to Iranian counterstrikes lest a major gamble appear to have backfired. The change will be profound, but it is impossible to predict what will happen next with any confidence. The struggle for survival of the Islamic Republic is ongoing.
