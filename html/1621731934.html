Title : U.S. President Donald Trump’s Impact on Israeli-Palestinian Peace Talks (October 2025)

Foreign Affairs - October 2026

Article :

Donald Trump’s visit to Israel in October 2025 was met with an almost royal reception by the Israeli public and political leaders, who saw him as a savior for brokering a deal to end the war in Gaza. During his speech to the Knesset, dramatic footage of the release of remaining living Israeli hostages played on TV channels across the country, emphasizing Trump’s ability to break through decades-long deadlocks in Israeli politics.

The Peace Index, a survey that has polled Israeli attitudes toward the Israeli-Palestinian conflict since 1994, found that Jewish Israelis, particularly those on the political right and center, were more willing to consider peace negotiations when they were told such negotiations bore Trump’s imprimatur. This “Trump effect” could have far-reaching implications, as it defies assumptions about Israeli public opinion and international mediators’ traditional roles.

The failure of the Israeli political imagination regarding the Israeli-Palestinian conflict has been two decades in the making. The violent collapse of peace processes, Hamas’s takeover of Gaza, repeated wars in Gaza, deliberate obstruction of diplomacy by both sides, and a generation of Israelis internalizing the conflict as a chronic rather than solvable issue have produced a deep political fatalism.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has been criticized for this inertia, having spent his career managing rather than resolving the conflict with Palestinians. The October 7, 2023 attack on Israel exposed this strategy’s failure, leaving Israelis vulnerable without a vision to bring the conflict to an end or leadership capable of articulating a coherent alternative to the status quo.

A joint Palestinian-Israeli Pulse poll conducted in July 2024 illustrated the depth of political fatalism after the attacks, with only 31 percent of Jewish Israelis agreeing that there is a diplomatic way out through a political agreement; 63 percent believed there was a military way out through force. Yet, Israelis dislike the status quo, acknowledging its costs and the need for change. However, they do not agree on an alternative.

Trump’s unconventional approach to diplomacy resonates with constituencies that normally reject negotiations outright due to his perceived fearlessness, forcefulness, and unconditional commitment to Israel. His popularity in Jewish Israeli society is unprecedented among recent U.S presidents, with 73 percent expressing confidence in Trump, particularly among self-defined right-wingers who now constitute over 60 percent of the Jewish public.

Trump presented Netanyahu with a ready-made plan outlining 20 points for peace, making it difficult for an embattled prime minister to turn against a foreign leader popular among Israelis and not bound by domestic politics’ constraints. Trump’s willingness to defy diplomatic norms has created a psychological shortcut for Israelis who ordinarily distrust negotiation, viewing the plan as “Trump’s plan” and therefore as a guarantee of security, stability, and enforceability.

To capitalize on this moment and translate the Trump effect into a pathway to peace, the administration must fully implement the 20-point plan and use it as a springboard to create a broader regional consensus that defines a clear endgame for the Palestinian-Israeli conflict. This requires a clear and detailed settlement addressing all contentious issues and providing strong security guarantees, framed and presented as Trump’s peace plan. U.S. policymakers must be directly involved in its implementation to ensure Israeli leaders comply and believe such a framework is both enforceable and politically survivable.

The administration must not drag its feet, as Trump may lose interest, pursue secondary political grudges, or get distracted by other foreign policy priorities. Washington must advance as many permanent steps as possible while Trump remains in office, aiming to establish a viable Palestinian governing body in Gaza, ensure its security, and initiate economic redevelopment initiatives by the end of his tenure in 2029. The successor administration will not have the benefit of Trump’s singular leeway among the Jewish Israeli public, but if Trump makes enough progress over the next three years, the plan may generate enough momentum to survive beyond 2029.
